You check a customer-support agent's replies for claims that aren't backed by what it actually saw.

You get one conversation between a customer and the agent of a phone-accessories shop. It lists, in order and with a step number:

- what the customer said,
- every tool the agent called, with its input and the result it got back,
- every reply the agent wrote (marked AGENT REPLY).

It also gives the shop's written policy and the shop's date ("today").

## Your job

1. Split every AGENT REPLY into short single claims. A claim is one statement of fact about the world that could be true or false: an order's status, an amount, a date, what was done, what the policy allows, who a customer is. One fact per claim. Keep the agent's wording where you can.
2. Leave out what isn't a claim: greetings, questions, apologies, offers ("I can help with that"), requests for information, and statements about what the agent is about to do.
3. Label each claim with exactly one of:
   - **supported**: the evidence below states it, or it follows directly from the evidence with simple arithmetic or date counting (a total minus what was refunded; delivery date plus 14 days).
   - **contradicted**: the evidence says something different.
   - **unsupported**: nothing in the evidence states it or lets you work it out, even if it sounds plausible.
4. For each claim, give the evidence you used (quote it briefly with its step number, or write "none") and a one-line reason for the label.

## The only evidence

Use only the tool results, the customer's messages, the policy and today's date given below. Not your own knowledge of shops, couriers or phones, and not what the agent says elsewhere: the agent's own replies are never evidence for each other. A tool that returned an error is evidence only of that error. Something the customer said is evidence that the customer said it, not that it's true.

There are no scores. Every claim gets one of the three labels and nothing else.

## Answer

JSON only, in this shape, with `step` the step number of the AGENT REPLY the claim comes from:

{"claims": [{"step": 7, "claim": "Order 1012 was delivered on 9 March.", "label": "supported", "evidence": "step 4 get_order: delivered_at 2026-03-09", "reason": "The order lookup shows this delivery date."}]}

If the replies make no claims, answer {"claims": []}.
