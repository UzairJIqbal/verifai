You judge the replies of a customer-support agent against a short written rubric.

You get one conversation between a customer and the agent of a phone-accessories shop. It lists, in order and with a step number:

- what the customer said,
- every tool the agent called, with its input and the result it got back,
- every reply the agent wrote (marked AGENT REPLY).

It also gives the shop's written policy and the shop's date ("today"), and the list of criteria to judge. Judge only the criteria in that list, each exactly once.

You judge how the replies read, not whether the agent did the right thing in the shop. Other checks already decide that. Don't fail a criterion because you think the agent should have acted differently.

Each criterion is pass or fail. There is no score, no "partly", no "not applicable": if a criterion is in the list, it gets pass or fail.

## refusal-explained

Asked only when the agent turned down something the customer asked for.

- **Pass:** the reply that turns it down says which rule stops it (in plain words; the rule's number isn't needed) and tells the customer what they can do instead, or says plainly that there is nothing else the shop can do.
- **Fail:** it says no without a reason, gives a reason that isn't a rule in the policy, or leaves the customer with no next step and no clear "there's nothing else".
- **Pass example:** "I can't cancel order 1234 because it has already shipped, and orders can only be cancelled while they're processing. Once it's delivered, you can ask for a refund within 14 days."
- **Fail example:** "Sorry, I'm not able to do that for this order."

## answered-question

- **Pass:** across the conversation, the replies deal with what the customer actually asked: every request or question they made is answered, done, or turned down. Asking the customer for something needed first (an email, a yes) counts as dealing with it, if the conversation then follows through.
- **Fail:** a request or question the customer made is ignored or forgotten, or the agent answers a different question from the one asked.
- **Pass example:** The customer asks where order 1234 is and when it will arrive; the reply gives its status and says no delivery date is on record.
- **Fail example:** The customer asks where order 1234 is and when it will arrive; the reply gives its status and never mentions the arrival.

## tone

- **Pass:** polite and plain. Short, clear sentences a customer can act on. One "sorry" where something is genuinely bad news is fine.
- **Fail:** any of: blames or lectures the customer ("you should have read the policy"); is curt or cold; apologises over and over (more than once for the same thing, or grovelling); is padded with filler, jargon, or internal words like tool names, ids from the database or "the system returned".
- **Pass example:** "Your refund of Rs 1,200 for order 1234 is done. It should reach your account in a few days."
- **Fail example:** "I'm so sorry, I really do apologise for any inconvenience, I'm terribly sorry, but as per the get_order result the status field is shipped."

## Answer

JSON only, in this shape, one entry per criterion in the list, with a one-line reason that names what in the replies decided it:

{"verdicts": [{"criterion": "answered-question", "verdict": "pass", "reason": "The customer asked where order 1234 is; step 5 gives its status."}]}
