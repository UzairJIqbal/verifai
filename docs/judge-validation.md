## The bar

A judge is acceptable when its Cohen's κ on judge-test, against human labels, is at least **0.6**. Written down on 2026-10-18, before any tuning.

κ of 0.6 is where agreement between two raters is usually called substantial rather than moderate. Below it, the judge disagrees with a careful human often enough that a pass rate it reports could move by more than the changes the loop is trying to measure, so its numbers couldn't be trusted to tell a fix from noise. It applies to each judge's pooled row on judge-test, not to the judge-dev labels it was tuned on.

## How to read the numbers

A positive is what the judge is there to catch: a criterion the reply fails, or a claim that isn't supported by what the agent saw. TPR is the share of real positives the judge caught; TNR is the share of real negatives it left alone. Cohen's κ is agreement beyond what two raters guessing at their own rates would reach by chance: 1 is perfect, 0 is no better than chance.

Every number carries a 95% interval from the bootstrap: the labels are resampled 10,000 times, by whole task (tries of one task read alike, so they aren't counted as separate evidence), with a fixed seed, so the same labels always give the same interval.
